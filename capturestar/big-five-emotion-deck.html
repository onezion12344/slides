synthesis

Three distinct systems, one sloppy word

Construct Facets Psychological mechanism Question it answers
Emotional volatility N1 N3 N6 Threat-detection system hyper-reactivity "How much do you feel negative emotion?"
Emotional expressiveness E1 E6 Social reward and affiliation system "How much do you show emotion to others?"
Empathic emotionality A6 A3 Caregiving and cooperation system "How much are you moved by others?"

Why the domain level is the wrong tool. The 30-facet NEO-PI-R reveals that "emotional" in English conflates three psychologically independent mechanisms. The domain-level Big Five collapses them into noise — Extraversion's facets cancel out; Neuroticism's domain score hides which facets actually differ. To see what's really happening, you need the facet level.

The stereotype is signal + compression. The facet differences are real and cross-culturally robust (Schmitt et al., 2008, 55 nations). But everyday language takes seven specific facet effects, collapses them into "emotional," and erases the facets that point the other way (Angry Hostility, Assertiveness, Excitement-Seeking). Not false — selective.
what's missing

Two entire domains are silent —
and that's part of the story

Openness to Experience

No facet maps to "emotional." The sex differences here are in cognitive territory.

O3 "Feelings" (receptivity to inner emotional experience) is arguably emotional — but rarely framed that way in the stereotype. The gender-emotion narrative focuses on expression and distress, not emotional depth as a cognitive value.

Conscientiousness

The "control" domain. Sex differences are small and inconsistent.

C1
Competence — ♀ > ♂ (small)
C3
Dutifulness — ♀ > ♂ (small)
C2
Order — null

If "emotional" carried negative connotations of poor self-control, Conscientiousness would be relevant — but it doesn't show the pattern. The stereotype is specifically about affective traits, not control traits.

The selectivity is the point. Of 30 NEO-PI-R facets, only 7 feed the stereotype.
23 are irrelevant or contrary — and their silence reveals which "emotional" signals culture amplifies vs. ignores.
